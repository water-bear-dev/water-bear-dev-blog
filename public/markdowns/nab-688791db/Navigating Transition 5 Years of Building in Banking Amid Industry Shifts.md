Yesterday’s headlines announced significant workforce reductions across corporate and enabling functions at NAB—up to 2,500 roles across the organisation, with 1,500 slated before the end of the year.

As someone who has spent the last five years here, reading announcements like this brings a distinct mix of analytical detachment and immediate human reality. While consultation unfolds and many of us navigate uncertainty about whether our roles will be directly impacted, it feels like the right moment to step back and reflect: on what we built, how enterprise engineering actually works beneath the headlines, and where we go from here.

## The Enterprise Macro: Beyond the Headlines

When financial institutions announce large restructuring programs aimed at "simplifying operating models" and "reducing duplication," outside commentary often scrambles to point fingers—either blaming macroeconomic belt-tightening or framing it as wholesale AI replacement.

The ground-level reality is more nuanced:

**The Cost of Corporate Layering.** Over multi-year transformation cycles, enterprise organisations naturally accumulate layers of coordination, project management, and cross-functional overhead. Periodic reorganisations are institutional course-corrections to flatten decision loops and cut dependency chains.

**The Shift from AI Hype to Operational Efficiency.** Enterprise technology is past the era of running speculative proof-of-concepts just to show board-level innovation. Today’s mandate is ruthless prioritisation: internal developer tooling, self-service platforms, and governance that tangibly save time or reduce systemic risk.

**Execution Over Strategy.** High-level strategic slide decks don't keep core banking infrastructure resilient. What moves the needle is whether technical systems are decoupled, well-instrumented, and maintainable by lean, high-ownership engineering teams.

## Five Years of Building: What We Actually Delivered

Five years in banking technology is long enough to see buzzwords come and go, but the engineering fundamentals remain stubbornly constant.

Looking back across my time here, the work I am proudest of wasn't defined by the size of the budgets or the complexity of the architecture diagrams. It came down to practical utility:

**Bridging Platform and Developer Ergonomics.** Designing and delivering data platforms and AI tooling that internal teams actually wanted to use. When you build context engineering libraries and agentic frameworks that your own engineering colleagues adopt to cut through internal support friction, that’s where software feels genuinely impactful.

**Engineering in High-Stakes Environments.** Delivering generative systems, contact center intelligence, and automated pipelines inside strict security, authentication, and regulatory boundaries forces you to grow up quickly as an engineer. You learn that reliability, data lineage, and auditability aren't afterthoughts—they are the core product.

**The People in the Trenches.** The systems we delivered only worked because of the exceptional engineers, product partners, and analysts beside us. In an enterprise, technical problems are rarely just code problems; they are alignment problems. The camaraderie built while navigating production incidents, architectural reviews, and regulatory milestones is the most lasting takeaway from the past half-decade.

## Facing Uncertainty with Perspective

To pretend that restructuring announcements don't create anxiety would be dishonest. When you’ve poured years of craft into systems and teams, sitting in the ambiguity of whether your role is in scope is challenging.

Yet, technical resilience mirrors personal resilience:

**Control What You Can Control.** Corporate reorganisations happen at altitudes far above individual contributors. What remains entirely yours is the craft you sharpened, the integrity of your code, and the relationships you cultivated.

**Separating Role from Identity.** A corporate title or tenure at a single institution does not define an engineer's capability. The ability to decompose messy problems, architect dependable systems, and mentor colleagues translates anywhere.

**Gratitude for the Journey.** Regardless of how the consultation process concludes over the coming weeks, five years of tackling complex engineering challenges at this scale has been an invaluable chapter.

## What’s Ahead

The tech landscape is undergoing one of the most significant architectural shifts in decades. As platforms mature, the demand will only increase for engineers who can look past the hype, understand data sovereignty and security, and build practical, resilient systems.

To my colleagues navigating the same news: take pride in what we’ve built, support one another through the weeks ahead, and keep your sights firmly on the long horizon.

Onwards.
